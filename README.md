## Profile

Ph.D.-trained scientist in the biology of aging, with expertise in autophagy, neuroendocrine signaling, and organismal stress resilience. My dissertation work uncovered how neuropeptide signaling and autophagy interact across tissues to regulate stress resilience and lifespan in *C. elegans*. I’m pursuing scientist roles in longevity biotech where rigorous aging biology can be translated into real-world impact.

---

## Current Focus

- Mechanisms linking neuropeptide signaling to proteostasis and stress responses
- Cell non-autonomous regulation of autophagy across tissues
- Translational strategies for stress resilience and healthy aging

## Selected Experience

### Ph.D. Research Scientist, Biology of Aging (Aug 2020 – Dec 2025)
**Garrison Lab, Buck Institute / USC Leonard Davis**

- Identified nematocin (oxytocin/vasopressin homolog) as a suppressor of the heat shock response in *C. elegans*; first-author manuscript in preparation.
- Built imaging and analysis pipelines to quantify age- and tissue-specific autophagy and HSF-1 activation.
- Built RNA-seq differential expression/enrichment workflow in R and identified **42 DE genes** after heat shock, including **1.9x upregulation of sqst-1**.
- Generated **17 novel *C. elegans* strains**; established lifespan/thermotolerance/RNAi workflows and validated autophagy-dependence of survival phenotypes.
- President, Buck Institute Multicultural Trainee Association (2024–2025): led professional-development and outreach programming.

### Staff Research Associate II, Stem Cell Aging (Aug 2019 – Aug 2020)
**Brack Lab, UCSF**

- Isolated and characterized satellite cells from murine skeletal muscle to study age-associated stem-cell function.

### Research Associate II, Radiobiology R&D (Feb 2017 – Aug 2019)
